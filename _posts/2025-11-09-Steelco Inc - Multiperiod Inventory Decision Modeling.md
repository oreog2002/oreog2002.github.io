# Executive Summary <a name="summary-main"></a>

### Introduction & Objective <a name="summary-intro"></a>

GCM Consulting has been engaged to develop a profit-maximizing
production plan for the year 2025. The objective is to establish an
optimal schedule that effectively addresses fluctuating monthly demand,
constraints on raw material procurement, quality standards, and the
significant costs associated with inventory management (\$40/ton) and
backorders (up to \$1,000/ton).

<br> <br>

### Analysis and Methodology <a name="summary-analysis"></a>

Our team has employed a linear programming model using IBM ILOG CPLEX to
analyze Steelco's operational data, including demand forecasts, cost
structures, and material limitations. This model has enabled us to
identify the optimal 12-month schedule for purchasing, production, and
inventory management that maximizes profit.

<br> <br>

### Key Findings: Projected Profit Increase of \$3.54 Million <a name="summary-findings"></a>

The model's output indicates a strategic pathway toward achieving a
total profit of \$63,543,000 for the year 2025:

- **Optimized Plan Profit:** \$63,543,000
- **Status Quo Projection:** \$60,000,000
- **Profit Increase:** \$3,540,000 (a 5.9% increase)

This notable increase is the result of a calculated approach to cost
management. The model demonstrates that by accepting \$1.05 million in
forecasted inventory and backorder costs, there is an opportunity to
allocate limited raw materials toward higher-margin products, ultimately
leading to a net profit gain of \$3.54 million.

<br> <br>

# Technical Report <a name="technical-report"></a> {#technical-report}

### Introduction <a name="introduction"></a> {#introduction}

This report presents a comprehensive, profit-maximizing production plan
for Steelco Inc. for the 2025 calendar year. The company faced a complex
operational challenge: scheduling the production of five steel beam
types to meet fluctuating monthly demand while navigating a series of
financial and logistical constraints. These constraints include strict
monthly limits on purchasing new and recycled steel, minimum quality
requirements for each product, and significant financial penalties for
either holding excess inventory or incurring backorders. The objective
of this analysis was to use deterministic operations research methods to
develop an optimal solution that maximizes total profit, aiming to
surpass the current \$60,000,000 projection.

Using IBM's ILOG CPLEX Optimization Studio, we developed and solved a
linear programming model to address this problem. The optimal solution
found by this model achieves a total profit of \$63,543,000, which
represents a \$3.54 million (5.9%) increase over the status quo. This
benefit is realized by implementing a dynamic production schedule that
strategically uses material scarcity to its advantage. The plan dictates
the precise, most profitable course of action for every month, including
when to pre-build inventory and when to intentionally incur (less
costly) backorders to free up materials for higher-margin products.

The primary recommendations derived from this analysis are twofold.
First, we recommend the immediate adoption of the detailed 12-month
purchasing and production schedule generated by the model. Second, we
recommend that the management pursues strategic initiatives to increase
the monthly purchase limits for raw materials, as this was identified as
the primary bottleneck limiting even greater profitability. This report
provides a detailed breakdown of the problem, the mathematical model, a
full analysis of the results, and a concluding summary.

<br> <br>

### Description <a name="description"></a> {#description}

This project addresses the development of a 12-month, profit-maximizing
production plan for Steelco Inc. The company manufactures five types of
steel beams: I, W, U, H, and L. The objective is to create an optimized
work schedule for the 12 months of 2025 that maximizes total profit,
improving upon the company's status quo expectation of approximately
\$60,000,000 in profits.

The model must account for the following operational parameters,
constraints, and costs:

- **Raw Materials:** Beams are manufactured from two types of steel:

  - **Recycled Steel:** Costs \$400/ton, but only 80% of this material
    is usable. Purchases are limited to 6,000 tons per month.

  - **New Steel:** Costs \$600/ton, with a purchase limit of 5,000 tons
    per month.

- **Quality Requirements:** To meet quality standards, a minimum
  percentage of each beam must be manufactured from new steel: 40% for
  I-beams, 20% for W-beams, 60% for U-beams, 10% for H-beams, and 25%
  for L-beams.

- **Revenue and Demand:** The selling price for each beam type and the
  forecasted monthly demand for 2025 are provided in external data
  tables.

- **Operational Costs:**

  - **Inventory Cost:** A cost of \$40/ton is incurred for any product
    held in inventory from one month to the next.

  - **Backorder Penalty:** If demand is not met in a given month, a
    penalty is incurred, and the order is backlogged to be filled at a
    future time. The penalties are: \$100/ton for I-beams, \$300/ton for
    W-beams, \$250/ton for U-beams, \$600/ton for H-beams, and
    \$1000/ton for L-beams

- **Boundary Conditions:** A critical constraint of the model is that
  the company starts at the beginning of 2025 with zero inventory and no
  backorders. The 12-month plan must conclude at the end of 2025 in the
  same condition, with all inventory and backorders zeroed out.

<br> <br>

### Assumptions <a name="assumptions"></a> {#assumptions}

To successfully formulate this business problem as a linear program,
several key assumptions were made. These assumptions are standard for
deterministic operations research models and ensure the mathematical
validity of the solution:

1.  **Linearity:** All costs, revenues, and resource consumption are
    assumed to be linear. For example, the cost of 50 tons of steel is
    exactly 50 times the cost of one ton, and the profit from 50 beams
    is 50 times the profit of one beam. There are no "volume discounts"
    or "economies of scale" that would alter the per-unit cost or
    revenue.

2.  **Divisibility:** The decision variables (amounts to buy, produce,
    store, or backorder) are allowed to be fractional. For instance, it
    is acceptable for the model to recommend producing 1872.22 tons of a
    beam. This assumption is reasonable given that the unit of measure
    (tons) is already highly divisible.

3.  **Proportionality:** The resource usage is directly proportional to
    the activity level. For example, producing one ton of Beam I
    requires a fixed amount of new and recycled steel, and producing `x`
    tons requires exactly `x` times those amounts.

4.  **Additivity:** The total value of the objective function (profit)
    and the total resource consumption are the sum of their individual
    parts. For instance, the profits from I-beams, W-beams, U-beams,
    H-beams, and L-beams add together to form the total revenue, without
    any complex interactions between them.

5.  **Deterministic Nature:** All parameters used in the model are
    assumed to be known, fixed, and certain. This includes all costs,
    selling prices, and, most importantly, the 12-month demand forecast.
    The model does not account for any randomness or uncertainty in
    these values (e.g., a sudden spike in demand or a change in material
    costs).

6.  **Instantaneous Production:** It is assumed that all production for
    a given month can be completed within that month to meet demand. The
    model does not include lead times for raw material orders or
    multi-month production cycles.

7.  **Initial and Final Conditions:** The model explicitly assumes that
    the company starts the 2025 planning period with zero inventory and
    no outstanding backorders. Furthermore, the model is constrained to
    end 2025 in the exact same condition: all final inventory and
    backorder levels must be zero.

The Steelco production problem was formulated as a deterministic linear
programming model. The model's objective is to maximize total profit
over a 12-month horizon.

1.  **Indices and Sets:** To organize the model, we first define its

2.  **Parameters:** Parameters are the fixed, known values that serve as

    costs, prices, and limits—provided by Steelco. See Figures 3-4

    ![alt text](/img/posts/Figure-3.png "Figure 3")

    (*Figure 3*)

3.  **Decision Variables:** Decision variables are the unknown outputs
    of the model. These are the quantities that CPLEX will calculate to
    find the optimal solution. They represent the specific actions the
    company should take (how much to buy, produce, and store) in each
    time period. See Figure 5 below:

    ![alt text](/img/posts/Figure-5.png "Figure 5")

    (*Figure 5*)

4.  **Objective Function:** The objective function defines the primary
    goal of the model. In this case, the goal is to maximize the total
    profit. This is calculated as the total revenue from all sales,
    minus the total costs of materials, inventory, and backorder
    penalties incurred throughout the 12 months. See Figure 6 below:
    ![alt text](/img/posts/Figure-6.png "Figure 6")

    (*Figure 6*)

5.  **Constraints:** These are the rules, limitations, and operational
    realities that the final solution must obey. The model cannot
    produce a plan that violates any of these real-world limitations.
    These are applied to all products, materials, and time periods,
    where applicable.

    - **Material Purchase Limit:** This constraint ensures that the
        amount of material `j` bought in any month `t` does not exceed its
      monthly purchase limit `PL_j`. See Figure 7:
      ![alt text](/img/posts/Figure-7.png "Figure 7")

      (*Figure 7*)

    - **Material Usage:** This constraint links purchasing to
      production. It states that the total steel produced (summed across
      all products `i`) using material `j` cannot be more than the
      usable amount (Yield `Y_j` x amount bought) of material `j`
      purchased that month. See Figure 8: ![alt text](/img/posts/Figure-8.png "Figure 8")

      (*Figure 8*)

    - **Quality Requirement:** This constraint enforces the minimum
      quality standard. It says the amount of product `i` made from
      'New' steel must be greater than or equal to the required
      percentage `QR_i` of the total amount of product `i` produced
      (from both 'New' and 'Recycled' steel). See Figure 9:

      ![alt text](/img/posts/Figure-9.png "Figure 9")

      (*Figure 9*)

    - Inventory Balance: This is the core "flow" constraint that
      connects all months. It states that the "net inventory" (what you
      have minus what you owe) at the end of this month `t` must equal
      the net inventory from last month `t-1`, plus what you produced
      this month, minus what customers demanded this month. See Figure
      10:

      ![alt text](/img/posts/Figure-10.png "Figure 10")

      (*Figure 10*)

    - **Boundary Conditions:**

      1.  **Initial** `t=0`**:** `inv_{i,0} = 0` and `back_{i,0} = 0`.

          - This constraint sets the starting point, ensuring the
            company begins the year with zero inventory and no
            outstanding backorders. See Figure 11:

            ![alt text](/img/posts/Figure-11.png "Figure 11")

            (*Figure 11*)

      2.  **Final** `(t = 12)`**:** `inv_{i,12} = 0` and
          `back_{i,12} = 0`.

          - This constraint ensures the company ends the year "clean"
            with no leftover inventory or backorders, as specified in
            the project. See Figure 12:

            ![alt text](/img/posts/Figure-12.png "Figure 12")

            (*Figure 12*)

6.  **Post-Processing Script Explanation:** The execute block at the end
    of the .mod file is a post-processing script written in OPL's
    scripting language (which is based on JavaScript). This script runs
    after the CPLEX solver finds the optimal solution. Its purpose is to
    access the solution data and format it into a human-readable report.

    - **cplex.getObjValue():** This retrieves the final optimal value of
      the objective function, which is the total profit. See Figure 13:

      2.  It then loops through all products, materials, and time

      3.  It multiplies these solution values by their corresponding

### Results, Analysis, & Interpretation <a name="results-analysis"></a>

The linear programming model was successfully solved using the IBM ILOG
CPLEX Optimization Studio. The solver found an optimal solution that
maximizes total profit for 2025, satisfying all operational constraints.
This section presents this solution and provides a detailed analysis of
its business implications.

1.  **Overview of Optimal Solution:** The model identifies a maximum
    possible profit of \$63,543,000 for the 2025 fiscal year. This
    represents a \$3.54 million (5.9%) increase over the company's
    status quo projection of \$60,000,000. This total profit is the
    result of the following optimal cost and revenue breakdown (see
    Figure 17):

    ![alt text](/img/posts/Figure-17.png "Figure 17")

    (*Figure 17*)

2.  **Analysis and Interpretation of Results:** A technical audience
    should not only see what the solution is, but why it is optimal. The
    analysis reveals that the optimal plan is a complex strategy that
    balances three key factors: raw material scarcity, strategic cost
    incurrence, and dynamic production timing.

    - **Raw Material as the Primary Bottleneck:** The monthly purchase
      plan (see Figure 18) shows that the model buys the maximum-allowed
      5,000 tons of 'New' steel and 6,000 tons of 'Recycled' steel in 11
      of the 12 months. This proves that Steelco's operation is
      resource-constrained. The primary limiting factor on profitability
      is not production capacity, but the inability to purchase more raw
      materials. The only reason purchases drop in Month 12 is that the
      Final Conditions constraint (forcing inventory to zero) prevents
      the model from buying materials it cannot immediately sell.

    - **The "Strategic" Use of Costs:** A key insight is that the model
      intentionally incurs \$1,052,000 in combined inventory and
      backorder penalties (\$512k + \$540k). This is not a failure; it
      is a core part of the profit-maximizing strategy. The model has
      mathematically determined that it is more profitable to pay these
      penalties than to attempt a perfect "just-in-time" (JIT)
      production schedule.

      1.  **Strategic Backordering (e.g., I-Beams):** In Month 1, the
          model chooses to backorder 2,500 tons of I-Beams (see Figure
          21) and pay the \$100/ton penalty. It does this because the
              limited materials available in Month 1 are more profitably
              used to produce other beams (like U, H, and L). It
              is "smarter" to pay the I-Beam penalty and use the scarce
              resources on higher-margin products.
      2.  **Strategic Pre-Building (e.g., U-Beams):** In Month 2, the
          model produces 3,200 tons of U-Beams when demand is only 2,400
          (see Figure 19). It chooses to pay the \$40/ton inventory cost
          to store the extra 800 tons (see Figure 20). This implies that
          the model identified "spare" capacity in Month 2 and that
          building the beams then (and paying to store them) is cheaper
          than trying to build them in a more constrained future month.

    - **A Dynamic "Push-Pull" Strategy:** The production plan is not a
      simple "produce-to-demand" schedule. It is a dynamic mix of
      Just-in-Time (JIT), pre-building, and backordering.

      1.  **JIT:** For W-Beams in Month 3, demand is 900 tons. The model
          starts with 0 inventory, produces exactly 900 tons, and ends
          with 0 inventory.

      2.  **Dynamic Balancing:** The fractional production numbers
          (e.g., 1872.222 tons) demonstrate that the model is allocating
          every last usable ton of steel in the most optimal way
          possible, precisely balancing the cost equations against the
          quality constraints.

### Recommendations and Conclusion <a name="recommendations-conclusions"></a>

**Recommendations**

Based on the validated results of the linear programming model, we make
two primary recommendations—one tactical and one strategic.

1.  **Tactical Recommendation – Implement the Optimal Plan:** Our first
    recommendation is the immediate adoption of the 12-month optimal
    production and purchasing schedule presented in Section 5.3. This
    plan is mathematically proven to be the single most profitable
    course of action under the current operational constraints. Adhering
    to this schedule is the direct path to achieving the \$63,543,000
    profit, a \$3.54 million increase over the status quo. This plan
    provides the operations team with a precise, data-driven schedule
    for purchasing, production, and inventory management for every month
    of 2025.

2.  **Strategic Recommendation – Increase Raw Material Purchase
    Limits:** The model's results conclusively identify that the primary
    bottleneck on profitability is the monthly limit on raw material
    purchases. For 11 of the 12 months, the optimal solution purchased
    100% of the available 'New' and 'Recycled' steel. This means profit
    is not limited by production capacity, but by material scarcity. We
    strongly recommend that the management explores all avenues to
    expand these supply lines, particularly for the cost-effective
    80%-yield recycled steel. Securing even a small increase in these
    monthly limits will result in a direct and significant increase in
    total profit.

**Conclusion**

This analysis successfully modeled Steelco's complex 2025
production-planning problem as a deterministic linear program. The
optimal solution found by CPLEX provides a detailed, actionable plan
that balances resource scarcity, production requirements, and competing
costs to achieve a maximum profit of \$63.54 million.

The model revealed that the most profitable strategy is not to simply
meet demand, but to actively manage material constraints through a
dynamic mix of just-in-time production, strategic pre-building, and
calculated backordering. By implementing the provided schedule and
addressing the identified raw material bottleneck, Steelco Inc. can
confidently exceed its financial projections for 2025.
